How I got here

I didn't get into research in a straight line. I got here through the data most people would rather not deal with.

My first few years were on the practical side of machine learning: the pipelines, models and dashboards that humanitarian programmes at UNICEF, UNEP and CCAC actually relied on. That work left me with a habit I still keep. I don't trust a number until I've tried to break it.

Over time that habit turned into research. I did it alongside a full-time job, without a lab or funding, and taught myself three areas that don't usually overlap: computer vision, reinforcement learning and medical imaging. I ask the same thing of all of them. Does the model hold up once the data gets messy?

GreenMind, my first published paper, is where that question gets specific: a plant-disease model that scores 99% in the lab but only 29% in the field, and the slow work of closing that gap. I'm not attached to an institution. I just think research should be reproducible, measured honestly, and open for anyone to check. This is where I keep that work.

Research & publications

● Published, open access

GreenMind: explainable plant identification & disease screening

Preprint on Zenodo, 2026. DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23905344

An open system that identifies plant species and checks leaves for disease from a single photo, explains each prediction with class-activation maps, and reports something most papers skip: how well it does on photos it has never seen. Using a temporal split of iNaturalist data and a lab-to-field test for leaf disease, it shows a 70-point drop from lab to field, and four practical ways to narrow it. All the code, data splits and per-image predictions are public.

Reinforcement learning

QRC with expected eligibility traces

with Prof. Peter Vamplew, Federation University Australia

Extending QRC(λ) streaming deep RL with expected eligibility traces; multi-seed, 5M-step runs across five MinAtar environments on the Swinburne supercomputer, in JAX.

Medical imaging

PI-CAI prostate lesion segmentation

with Prof. Reyer Zwiggelaar, Aberystwyth University, UK

Benchmarking ITUNet, SwinUNETR, U-Mamba and nnU-Net under the PI-CAI protocol, with five-fold experiments and standardized held-out evaluation (AUROC / AP).

Computer vision

Scoring children's hand-drawn shapes

with Prof. Nam, ICT Research Centre / UC Lab

Ordinal scoring (0 to 6) of BOT-2 shapes with ViT-S/16 and MobileViT-S, linear probing and DiffuseMix / CutMix / MixUp, evaluated with Quadratic Weighted Kappa under heavy class imbalance.
